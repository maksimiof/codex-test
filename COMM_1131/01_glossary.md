# COMM 1131 — Glossary of Key Terms

> Alphabetical reference for all major vocabulary in Sex, Relationships, and Communication.

---

## A

**Affirmative Consent**
Active, ongoing, voluntary, and informed agreement to engage in sexual activity. Silence or absence of resistance does not equal consent.

**Asexuality**
A sexual orientation characterized by experiencing little or no sexual attraction to others; distinct from celibacy (a choice) or low libido.

**Attachment Theory**
A psychological framework (Bowlby, Ainsworth) proposing that early caregiver relationships shape internal "working models" that influence adult intimacy and communication styles. Three primary adult attachment styles: *secure*, *anxious-preoccupied*, and *dismissive-avoidant* (plus *fearful-avoidant*).

**Autonomy**
The right and ability to make informed, self-directed decisions about one's own body, identity, and relationships without coercion.

---

## B

**Bisexuality**
Sexual attraction to people of one's own gender and other genders.

**Body Image**
A person's subjective perception of and feelings about their physical appearance; heavily influenced by media, culture, and interpersonal feedback.

**Boundaries**
Explicit or implicit limits a person sets to protect their physical, emotional, and sexual well-being; communicated verbally and nonverbally.

---

## C

**Channel (Communication)**
The medium through which a message travels (e.g., face-to-face speech, text, nonverbal cues).

**Cisgender**
A person whose gender identity aligns with the sex they were assigned at birth.

**Cohabitation**
Living together in an intimate partnership without legal marriage.

**Coming Out**
The process by which a person discloses a LGBTQ+ identity to others; an ongoing, context-dependent process rather than a single event.

**Communication Apprehension**
Anxiety associated with real or anticipated communication in interpersonal, group, or public contexts.

**Compulsory Heterosexuality**
Adrienne Rich's concept describing how heterosexuality is assumed as the "default" and enforced through cultural norms, limiting individual choice.

**Conflict**
A perceived incompatibility of goals, needs, or values between relationship partners; not inherently destructive.

**Consent** *(see Affirmative Consent)*

**Contraception**
Methods used to prevent pregnancy; includes barrier, hormonal, intrauterine, and behavioral methods. Pharmacists are key access points for many OTC contraceptives.

---

## D

**Decode**
The process by which a receiver interprets a message.

**Disclosure** *(see Self-Disclosure)*

**Dyadic Communication**
Two-person (one-on-one) communication; the dominant mode in intimate relationships.

---

## E

**Emotional Intelligence (EI)**
The ability to recognize, understand, manage, and effectively use emotions in interpersonal contexts (Goleman).

**Encode**
The process by which a sender translates thoughts into a transmittable message.

**Erotic**
Related to sexual desire or arousal; often used to describe stimuli, imagery, or communication that arouses sexual interest.

---

## F

**Feedback**
The receiver's response to a message, which informs the sender whether the message was understood.

**Feminism**
A range of social, political, and philosophical movements and ideologies that share a common goal: to define, establish, and achieve political, economic, and social equality for all genders.

---

## G

**Gender**
A social construct encompassing identity, expression, roles, and expectations associated with being a man, woman, or nonbinary person; distinct from biological sex.

**Gender Binary**
The classification of gender into two distinct, opposite categories (man/woman); critiqued for erasing nonbinary and intersex experiences.

**Gender Expression**
The external presentation of gender through clothing, behavior, hairstyle, etc.; may or may not align with gender identity.

**Gender Identity**
A person's internal, deeply held sense of their own gender.

**Gender Nonconforming / Nonbinary**
Umbrella terms for gender identities that don't fit neatly into the man/woman binary.

---

## H

**Harassment (Sexual)**
Unwanted verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature that creates a hostile or offensive environment.

**Heteronormativity**
The assumption that heterosexuality is the normal or default sexual orientation, and that gender roles are fixed and binary.

**Homophobia**
Fear, discomfort, or prejudice toward gay, lesbian, or bisexual individuals; manifests as individual attitudes and systemic discrimination.

**Human Papillomavirus (HPV)**
The most common STI; certain strains cause genital warts and cervical/oropharyngeal cancers. Vaccine-preventable. ⚕️ *PharmD Connection: pharmacists administer HPV vaccines.*

---

## I

**Interpersonal Communication**
The exchange of messages between people who are in relationship with each other and who influence each other through the process.

**Intersectionality**
Kimberlé Crenshaw's framework describing how overlapping social identities (race, gender, class, sexuality, disability) create unique experiences of privilege and oppression.

**Intersex**
Born with reproductive or sexual anatomy that doesn't fit typical definitions of male or female.

**Intimate Partner Violence (IPV)**
A pattern of abusive behaviors used by one partner to gain power and control over another; includes physical, sexual, emotional, and economic abuse.

---

## J

**Jealousy**
An emotional response to a perceived threat to a valued relationship; influenced by attachment style and cultural norms.

---

## K

**Kinsey Scale**
A 0–6 scale developed by Alfred Kinsey to describe sexual orientation as a continuum from exclusively heterosexual (0) to exclusively homosexual (6).

---

## L

**LGBTQ+**
Acronym for Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual, Transgender, Queer/Questioning, and other sexual/gender minority identities (the "+" is inclusive of all others).

**Libido**
Sexual drive or desire; influenced by hormones (testosterone, estrogen), neurotransmitters, psychological factors, medications, and health status. ⚕️ *PharmD Connection: many medications (SSRIs, antihypertensives) affect libido as a side effect.*

**Love Languages**
Gary Chapman's framework proposing five primary ways people express and receive love: Words of Affirmation, Acts of Service, Receiving Gifts, Quality Time, Physical Touch.

---

## M

**Masturbation**
Self-stimulation for sexual pleasure; clinically associated with no health harms and some health benefits.

**Media Literacy**
The ability to critically analyze, evaluate, and create messages in a wide variety of media formats; important for deconstructing sexual norms in media.

**Monogamy**
A relationship structure in which a person has only one romantic or sexual partner at a time.

---

## N

**Noise (Communication)**
Anything that interferes with the transmission or reception of a message (physical, psychological, semantic, or physiological).

**Nonverbal Communication**
Transmission of meaning through body language, facial expressions, eye contact, touch, proxemics, and paralanguage.

---

## O

**Objectification**
The act of treating a person as an object for one's own purposes, especially sexual purposes; associated with media representation and harassment.

**Orgasm**
The peak of sexual excitement characterized by involuntary muscular contractions and intense pleasure; occurs in multiple phases (Masters & Johnson model).

---

## P

**Paralanguage**
Vocal cues beyond words: tone, pitch, rate, volume, pauses; critical in communicating emotion and intent.

**Paraphilia**
An intense and persistent sexual attraction to atypical objects, situations, or individuals. Clinically significant only if causing distress or involving non-consent.

**Patriarchy**
A social system in which men hold primary power and women are largely excluded from positions of power; shapes gender norms and relationship dynamics.

**Polyamory**
The practice of or desire for intimate relationships with more than one partner, with the knowledge and consent of all partners involved.

**Power (Relational)**
The ability of one partner to influence the other's behavior; can be balanced or imbalanced.

**Proxemics**
The study of how people use and perceive physical space in communication (intimate, personal, social, public zones).

---

## Q

**Queer**
An umbrella term for sexual and gender minorities; reclaimed from a historical slur; also used as an academic framework (Queer Theory).

**Queer Theory**
An academic framework that deconstructs fixed categories of sexuality and gender, highlighting their socially constructed and fluid nature.

---

## R

**Rape Culture**
A sociological concept describing an environment in which sexual violence is normalized, excused, or even tacitly condoned through prevalent attitudes, norms, and media representations.

**Relationship Maintenance**
Behaviors and communication strategies used to sustain and strengthen a relationship over time.

**Reproductive Health**
A state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being in all matters relating to the reproductive system.

---

## S

**Safe Sex**
Practices that reduce the risk of STIs and unintended pregnancy (condoms, PrEP, regular testing). ⚕️ *PharmD Connection: pharmacists counsel on barrier methods, emergency contraception, and PrEP.*

**Schema**
A mental framework that organizes and interprets information; influences how people interpret messages and relationships.

**Self-Concept**
The overall image or perception a person has of themselves; includes self-esteem and self-efficacy.

**Self-Disclosure**
Voluntarily sharing personal information with another person; essential to intimacy development (Social Penetration Theory).

**Sex**
Biological classification (male, female, intersex) typically based on chromosomes, hormones, and anatomy; distinct from gender.

**Sexism**
Prejudice, stereotyping, or discrimination based on sex or gender.

**Sexual Coercion**
Unwanted sexual activity that results from pressure, manipulation, threats, or force; distinct from but related to assault.

**Sexual Harassment** *(see Harassment)*

**Sexual Health**
A state of physical, emotional, mental, and social well-being related to sexuality; includes access to information, positive relationships, and freedom from coercion.

**Sexual Orientation**
Enduring pattern of emotional, romantic, and/or sexual attraction to men, women, both, neither, or other genders.

**Sexual Response Cycle**
Masters & Johnson's four-phase model: *Excitement → Plateau → Orgasm → Resolution*. ⚕️ *PharmD Connection: overlaps with PHSC 2303 Human Physiology 2.*

**Socialization**
The process by which individuals learn and internalize the norms, values, and behaviors of their culture, including sexual scripts.

**Social Penetration Theory**
Altman & Taylor's theory describing relationship development as progressive self-disclosure, moving from shallow/broad to deep/narrow, like layers of an onion.

**STI (Sexually Transmitted Infection)**
An infection transmitted through sexual contact; includes HIV, chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis, herpes, HPV, and others. ⚕️ *PharmD Connection: pharmacists test, treat, and counsel on many STIs.*

---

## T

**Toxic Masculinity**
Cultural norms that associate masculinity with dominance, emotional suppression, and aggression; harmful to men and those around them.

**Transgender**
An umbrella term for people whose gender identity differs from the sex assigned at birth.

**Transphobia**
Fear, discomfort, or prejudice toward transgender or gender-nonconforming individuals.

---

## U

**Uncertainty Reduction Theory**
Berger & Calabrese's theory positing that people are motivated to reduce uncertainty about others when first meeting them; drives initial communication behavior.

---

## V

**Verbal Communication**
The use of words (spoken or written) to convey meaning.

---

## W

**Whorfianism (Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis)**
The idea that the language we use shapes our perceptions of reality; relevant to how language about gender and sexuality shapes cultural norms.

---

## Z

**Zone of Proximal Development** *(Vygotsky)*
While primarily an education concept, it applies to relationships: growth happens in the space between what one can handle alone and what is possible with a supportive partner.
