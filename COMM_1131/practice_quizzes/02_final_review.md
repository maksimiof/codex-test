## Section 2: True or False — Explain Your Reasoning

**11. True or False: Silence during a sexual encounter constitutes affirmative consent.**

> **Answer: FALSE.** Affirmative consent requires active, voluntary, and enthusiastic agreement. Silence may indicate fear, freezing (a trauma response), or discomfort — not willingness. The FRIES model specifies that consent must be "freely given" and "enthusiastic," neither of which silence satisfies.

---

**12. True or False: Older adults rarely engage in sexual activity and therefore do not need STI counseling.**

> **Answer: FALSE.** Sexual activity continues throughout the lifespan for many individuals. STI rates among older adults (particularly 65+) have been *increasing*. Healthcare providers (including pharmacists) often fail to counsel older adults on STI prevention because of ageist assumptions. Older adults are also less likely to use condoms because pregnancy is no longer a concern. Pharmacists should provide age-appropriate STI counseling to all sexually active patients.

---

**13. True or False: Attachment styles are fixed and cannot change throughout adulthood.**

> **Answer: FALSE.** While early attachment experiences create "working models" that influence adult relationships, research shows that attachment styles can change through therapy, positive relationship experiences, and intentional self-awareness. Secure attachment can be *earned* through consistent, healthy relationships even for those with insecure early attachment.

---

**14. True or False: Polyamory and "open relationships" are the same thing.**

> **Answer: FALSE.** While both are forms of ethical non-monogamy, they differ. An *open relationship* typically involves a primary couple who may have outside *sexual* partners, often without romantic involvement. *Polyamory* involves multiple loving, romantic relationships where all partners are aware and consenting — it centers love and emotional connection, not just sexual freedom. Both require transparency and consent, but they are distinct structures.

## Section 5: PharmD Integration — Short Answer

**19. Name two classes of medications that commonly cause sexual dysfunction as a side effect, and describe the mechanism for each.**

> **Model Answer:**
> 1. **SSRIs (e.g., sertraline, fluoxetine):** Increase serotonin → inhibit dopaminergic and noradrenergic pathways → reduced libido, delayed or absent orgasm, and arousal difficulties. Serotonin also inhibits oxytocin release, affecting bonding.
> 2. **Antihypertensives (e.g., β-blockers like metoprolol; thiazide diuretics):** β-blockers reduce sympathetic activity needed for arousal and orgasm; may also cause depression, further reducing libido. Thiazides may decrease zinc levels, affecting testosterone synthesis.

**20. A patient asks you about the difference between Plan B® and Ella®. What would you tell them?**

> **Model Answer:** Both are emergency contraceptives that prevent pregnancy primarily by delaying or preventing ovulation — neither is an abortion pill. Plan B® (levonorgestrel) is available over the counter, no age restriction or prescription needed. It is most effective within 72 hours but can be used up to 120 hours; effectiveness decreases with time. Ella® (ulipristal acetate) requires a prescription and maintains better efficacy across the full 120-hour window, especially later in that window. Body weight may affect the efficacy of Plan B® more than Ella® in individuals over ~165–175 lbs. I'd recommend Ella® for maximum effectiveness if they can get a prescription in time, or Plan B® if they need it immediately over the counter.
