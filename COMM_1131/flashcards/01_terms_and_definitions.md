## Unit 1 — Communication Fundamentals

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Transactional model** | Model in which both parties are simultaneous senders and receivers; communication is dynamic and co-created |
| **Noise** | Anything that distorts or interferes with message transmission (physical, psychological, semantic, physiological) |
| **Feedback** | The receiver's response to a message |
| **Channel** | The medium through which a message travels |
| **Paralanguage** | Vocal cues beyond words: tone, pitch, rate, volume, pauses |
| **Proxemics** | Study of how people use and perceive physical space |
| **Intimate space (Hall)** | 0–18 inches; reserved for close partners and family |
| **Pseudolistening** | Appearing to listen while actually distracted or unengaged |
| **Active listening** | Fully attending to and engaging with a speaker's message |

## Unit 2 — Relationship Theories

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Social Penetration Theory** | Altman & Taylor; relationship depth increases through progressive self-disclosure ("onion" model) |
| **Breadth (Social Penetration)** | The range of topics discussed in a relationship |
| **Depth (Social Penetration)** | The level of intimacy/vulnerability in a topic |
| **Depenetration** | Withdrawal of self-disclosure as a relationship dissolves |
| **Uncertainty Reduction Theory** | Berger & Calabrese; people are motivated to reduce uncertainty about new acquaintances |
| **CLalt (Social Exchange Theory)** | Comparison Level for Alternatives; how your current relationship compares to perceived options |
| **Relational Dialectics** | Baxter & Montgomery; ongoing, irresolvable tensions in relationships |
| **Autonomy–Connection** | Dialectic: wanting closeness vs. independence |
| **Openness–Closedness** | Dialectic: wanting to share vs. wanting privacy |
| **Novelty–Predictability** | Dialectic: wanting excitement vs. stability |
| **Secure attachment** | Comfortable with both intimacy and autonomy; effective communicator |
| **Anxious-preoccupied attachment** | Craves closeness; fears abandonment; hypervigilant |
| **Dismissive-avoidant attachment** | Values independence; uncomfortable with emotional closeness |
| **Consummate love (Sternberg)** | Love with all three components: intimacy + passion + commitment |

## Unit 3 — Relationship Development & Types

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Knapp's Staircase** | Ten-stage model of relationship coming together (5 stages) and apart (5 stages) |
| **Differentiating (Knapp)** | First stage of coming apart; "I" replaces "we" |
| **Stonewalling (Knapp)** | Avoiding stage equivalent; shutting down communication |
| **Eros (Lee)** | Romantic, passionate love based on intense attraction |
| **Ludus (Lee)** | Game-playing, non-committal love |
| **Storge (Lee)** | Slow-developing, friendship-based love |
| **Pragma (Lee)** | Practical, logical partner selection (Ludus + Storge) |
| **Agape (Lee)** | Unconditional, selfless love (Eros + Storge) |
| **Mania (Lee)** | Obsessive, possessive love with jealousy and anxiety (Eros + Ludus) |
| **Polyamory** | Ethical non-monogamy with multiple loving relationships, with all partners' knowledge |
| **Duck's Grave-dressing phase** | Post-breakup narrative construction; making sense of the relationship's end |
| **Maintenance behaviors (Canary)** | Positivity, openness, assurances, social networks, sharing tasks |
| **Duluth Model** | Power and control wheel describing IPV tactics |

## Unit 4 — Gender, Sexuality, Identity

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Sex** | Biological classification (chromosomes, hormones, anatomy) |
| **Gender** | Social/psychological identity; exists on a spectrum |
| **Gender expression** | External presentation of gender through clothing, behavior, etc. |
| **Sexual orientation** | Enduring pattern of emotional, romantic, sexual attraction |
| **Cisgender** | Gender identity matches sex assigned at birth |
| **Transgender** | Gender identity differs from sex assigned at birth |
| **Nonbinary** | Gender identity outside the man/woman binary |
| **Asexual** | Little or no sexual attraction |
| **Pansexual** | Attraction regardless of gender |
| **Kinsey Scale** | 0–6 continuum of sexual orientation |
| **Split Attraction Model** | Separates sexual attraction from romantic attraction |
| **Performativity (Butler)** | Gender is constituted through repeated acts, not an essential quality |
| **Cass Identity Model** | Six-stage model of LGBTQ+ identity development |
| **Intersex** | Born with sex characteristics outside typical male/female definitions |
| **Two-Spirit** | Third gender recognized in many Indigenous North American cultures |

## Unit 5 — Consent and Ethics

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Affirmative consent** | Active, voluntary, ongoing, informed agreement to sexual activity |
| **FRIES model** | Freely given, Reversible, Informed, Enthusiastic, Specific |
| **Sexual coercion** | Unwanted sexual activity resulting from pressure, manipulation, or threats |
| **Rape culture** | Social environment normalizing, trivializing, or condoning sexual violence |
| **Bystander intervention** | Taking action to interrupt potential sexual harassment or assault |
| **3 D's (bystander)** | Direct, Distract, Delegate |
| **Intimate Partner Violence (IPV)** | Pattern of abusive behaviors to gain power/control over a partner |
| **Quid pro quo harassment** | Sexual favor made a condition of employment or education |

## Unit 6 — Sexual Health

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Masters & Johnson model** | Four-phase sexual response: Excitement, Plateau, Orgasm, Resolution |
| **Kaplan's model** | Three-phase: Desire, Arousal, Orgasm (adds desire phase) |
| **Oxytocin** | "Bonding hormone" released during orgasm and touch |
| **LARC** | Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (IUD, implant) |
| **PrEP** | Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis; medication taken before HIV exposure to prevent infection |
| **PEP** | Post-Exposure Prophylaxis; must begin within 72 hours of HIV exposure |
| **Plan B®** | Levonorgestrel emergency contraceptive; OTC; effective up to 72–120 hours |
| **PLISSIT model** | Permission, Limited Information, Specific Suggestions, Intensive Therapy — sexual health counseling framework |
| **Chlamydia** | Most common STI; often asymptomatic; treated with azithromycin or doxycycline |
| **HPV** | Most common STI overall; vaccine-preventable (Gardasil 9®) |
| **Spectatoring** | Mental self-monitoring of appearance during sex; reduces sexual presence and satisfaction |

## Unit 7 — Media and Sexuality

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Cultivation Theory** | Gerbner; heavy TV viewing cultivates perception of reality matching TV content |
| **Sexual script** | Socially learned blueprint for how sexual interactions are supposed to occur |
| **Objectification Theory** | Fredrickson & Roberts; women's bodies treated as objects → self-objectification |
| **Self-objectification** | Internalizing the observer's gaze; monitoring one's own appearance |
| **Heteronormativity** | Assumption that heterosexuality is the normal or default orientation |
| **Phubbing** | Phone-snubbing during in-person interaction; harms relationship quality |
| **Social comparison** | Evaluating oneself by comparison to others (amplified by social media) |

## Unit 8 — Diversity and Intersectionality

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Intersectionality** | Crenshaw; overlapping social identities create unique experiences of privilege/oppression |
| **Minority Stress Model** | Meyer; chronic stress from stigma/discrimination explains LGBTQ+ health disparities |
| **Trauma-informed care** | Healthcare approach that recognizes and responds to the effects of trauma |
| **Microaggression** | Brief, everyday slights that communicate hostile or demeaning messages to marginalized groups |
| **Health disparities** | Preventable differences in disease burden, injury, or access to care among groups |
| **Ableism** | Discrimination or prejudice against people with disabilities |
| **Hypersexualization** | Excessive sexual stereotyping applied to a racial or ethnic group in media or culture |
