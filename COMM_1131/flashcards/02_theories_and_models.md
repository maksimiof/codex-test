## Communication Theories

| Theory / Model | Key Author(s) | Core Idea | Key Terms |
|----------------|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Shannon-Weaver Transmission Model** | Shannon & Weaver (1949) | Linear model: Sender → Encode → Message → Channel → Decode → Receiver; includes noise | Sender, encode, channel, decode, feedback, noise |
| **Transactional Model** | Various | Communication is simultaneous, dynamic, and co-created by both parties in a shared context | Simultaneous roles, shared field of experience, context |
| **Social Penetration Theory** | Altman & Taylor (1973) | Relationships deepen through progressive self-disclosure from superficial to intimate ("onion model") | Breadth, depth, depenetration |
| **Uncertainty Reduction Theory** | Berger & Calabrese (1975) | Strangers are motivated to reduce uncertainty through passive, active, and interactive strategies | Passive/active/interactive strategies |
| **Relational Dialectics Theory** | Baxter & Montgomery (1988) | Relationships are characterized by ongoing, irresolvable tensions that partners must continuously manage | Autonomy–Connection, Openness–Closedness, Novelty–Predictability |
| **Social Exchange Theory** | Thibaut & Kelley (1959) | Relationships evaluated as cost-benefit analyses; satisfaction depends on CL and CLalt | Rewards, costs, CL, CLalt |

## Love and Relationship Theories

| Theory / Model | Key Author(s) | Core Idea | Key Terms |
|----------------|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Triangular Theory of Love** | Sternberg (1986) | Love has three components; different combinations produce different types of love | Intimacy, passion, commitment; consummate, companionate, infatuation |
| **Color Wheel of Love** | Lee (1973) | Six love styles analogous to primary and secondary colors | Eros, ludus, storge, pragma, mania, agape |
| **Attachment Theory** (adult) | Bowlby; Hazan & Shaver | Early attachment shapes adult relationship patterns | Secure, anxious, avoidant, fearful-avoidant |
| **Knapp's Staircase Model** | Knapp (1978) | Relationships move through 5 stages coming together and 5 stages coming apart | Initiating, experimenting, intensifying, integrating, bonding; differentiating, circumscribing, stagnating, avoiding, terminating |
| **Duck's Dissolution Model** | Duck (1982) | Four (later five) phases of relationship breakup | Intrapsychic, dyadic, social, grave-dressing, (resurrection) |
| **Gottman's Four Horsemen** | Gottman (1994) | Four communication patterns that predict relationship dissolution | Criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling |

## Gender and Sexuality Models

| Theory / Model | Key Author(s) | Core Idea | Key Terms |
|----------------|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Performativity of Gender** | Butler (1990) | Gender is constituted through repeated acts, not a fixed essential quality | Performative, Gender Trouble |
| **Doing Gender** | West & Zimmerman (1987) | Gender is accomplished in everyday social interactions | Accountability, situated conduct |
| **Kinsey Scale** | Kinsey (1948) | Sexual orientation is a continuum from 0 (exclusively heterosexual) to 6 (exclusively homosexual) | Continuum, 0–6 scale |
| **Cass Identity Model** | Cass (1979) | Six stages of LGBTQ+ identity development | Confusion, comparison, tolerance, acceptance, pride, synthesis |
| **Social Constructionism** | Berger & Luckmann | Gender and sexuality are products of social processes and cultural norms, not fixed biological facts | Social construction, cultural norms |
| **Queer Theory** | Butler, Foucault | Deconstructs fixed categories of sexuality and gender; challenges heteronormativity | Heteronormativity, binary, deconstruction |

## Sexuality and Health Models

| Theory / Model | Key Author(s) | Core Idea | Key Terms |
|----------------|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Sexual Response Cycle** | Masters & Johnson (1966) | Four physiological phases of sexual response | Excitement, plateau, orgasm, resolution, refractory period |
| **Three-Phase Model** | Kaplan (1979) | Adds desire phase to Masters & Johnson model | Desire, arousal, orgasm |
| **Sexual Script Theory** | Simon & Gagnon (1973) | Sexual behavior is learned through cultural, interpersonal, and intrapsychic scripts | Cultural scenario, interpersonal script, intrapsychic script |
| **PLISSIT Model** | Annon (1976) | Four-level framework for addressing sexual concerns in clinical settings | Permission, Limited Information, Specific Suggestions, Intensive Therapy |

## Media and Culture Theories

| Theory / Model | Key Author(s) | Core Idea | Key Terms |
|----------------|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Cultivation Theory** | Gerbner (1970s) | Heavy media consumption shapes viewers' perceptions of social reality | Mean world syndrome, heavy viewers, mainstreaming |
| **Social Learning Theory** | Bandura | We learn behaviors (including sexual) by observing and modeling others in media | Modeling, vicarious reinforcement, self-efficacy |
| **Objectification Theory** | Fredrickson & Roberts (1997) | Women's bodies are treated as objects for others' pleasure → self-objectification | Self-objectification, spectatoring, body shame |
| **Compulsory Heterosexuality** | Adrienne Rich (1980) | Heterosexuality is enforced as the default, limiting choices | Institutionalized heterosexuality, heteronormativity |

## Social Justice and Health Frameworks

| Theory / Model | Key Author(s) | Core Idea | Key Terms |
|----------------|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Intersectionality** | Crenshaw (1989) | Overlapping social identities create unique experiences of privilege and oppression | Matrix of domination, identity, privilege |
| **Minority Stress Model** | Meyer (2003) | Chronic stress from stigma and discrimination explains LGBTQ+ health disparities | Chronic stress, stigma, distal/proximal stressors |
| **Trauma-Informed Care** | SAMHSA | Healthcare approach that recognizes the pervasive impact of trauma | Safety, trust, empowerment, collaboration, cultural sensitivity |
| **FRIES Model of Consent** | Planned Parenthood | Consent must be Freely given, Reversible, Informed, Enthusiastic, and Specific | FRIES, affirmative consent |
| **Principled Negotiation** | Fisher & Ury (*Getting to Yes*) | Separate people from problems; focus on interests; generate options; use objective criteria | Interest-based negotiation, BATNA |
