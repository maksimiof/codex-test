# Study Guide 1 — Communication Theories in Relationships

---

## 1. The Communication Process

### Shannon-Weaver Transmission Model (1949)
A linear model showing how messages travel from sender to receiver.

```
Sender → Encode → Message → Channel → Decode → Receiver
                        ↑
                      Noise
```

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Sender** | Originates the message |
| **Encode** | Translates thought into symbols (words, gestures) |
| **Channel** | Medium of transmission (voice, text, touch) |
| **Decode** | Receiver interprets the symbols |
| **Receiver** | Destination of the message |
| **Noise** | Anything that distorts the message |
| **Feedback** | Receiver's response back to sender |

> **Limitation:** Linear; doesn't capture the simultaneous, transactional nature of real communication.

---

### Transactional Model of Communication
People are *simultaneously* senders and receivers; communication is dynamic, ongoing, and co-created.

- Both parties influence each other continuously.
- Context (physical, social, historical, psychological) shapes meaning.
- "Noise" exists in multiple forms: **physical** (loud music), **psychological** (stress, bias), **semantic** (different word meanings), **physiological** (illness, fatigue).

## 2. Key Interpersonal Communication Theories

### Social Penetration Theory — Altman & Taylor (1973)
**Core Idea:** Relationships develop through progressive self-disclosure, moving from shallow to deep.

**The "Onion" Metaphor:**
```
Outer layer:  Superficial info (name, major, interests)
Middle layers: Personal opinions, values, goals
Inner core:   Deepest fears, secrets, core identity
```

- **Breadth:** Range of topics discussed
- **Depth:** Level of intimacy/vulnerability in a topic
- Relationship development: increase in both breadth AND depth over time
- **Depenetration:** As relationships dissolve, disclosure reverses

---

### Uncertainty Reduction Theory — Berger & Calabrese (1975)
**Core Idea:** When we meet strangers, we are motivated to *reduce uncertainty* about them to predict their behavior and feel comfortable.

**Three Strategies to Reduce Uncertainty:**
1. **Passive:** Observe the person without interaction
2. **Active:** Ask others about the person
3. **Interactive:** Direct conversation

**Axioms (key propositions):**
- Higher uncertainty → more verbal communication, more questions
- Higher uncertainty → less intimacy
- As uncertainty decreases → liking increases (usually)

---

### Relational Dialectics Theory — Baxter & Montgomery (1988)
**Core Idea:** Relationships are characterized by ongoing, irresolvable tensions (contradictions) that partners must continuously manage.

**Three Primary Dialectics:**

| Dialectic | Tension |
|-----------|---------|
| **Autonomy–Connection** | Wanting closeness vs. independence |
| **Openness–Closedness** | Wanting to share vs. wanting privacy |
| **Novelty–Predictability** | Wanting excitement/change vs. stability/routine |

**Management Strategies:**
- **Cyclic alternation:** Take turns honoring each pole
- **Segmentation:** Each pole honored in different domains
- **Neutralization:** Compromise between poles
- **Reframing:** Redefine the tension so it's no longer contradictory

---

### Social Exchange Theory — Thibaut & Kelley (1959)
**Core Idea:** People evaluate relationships as economic exchanges, weighing costs against rewards.

**Key Concepts:**
- **Rewards:** Positive outcomes (companionship, support, pleasure)
- **Costs:** Negative outcomes (time, stress, conflict)
- **Comparison Level (CL):** Standard against which you evaluate relationship satisfaction
- **Comparison Level for Alternatives (CLalt):** How current relationship compares to perceived alternatives
- **Outcome = Rewards − Costs**
  - If Outcome > CL: satisfied
  - If Outcome > CLalt: likely to stay

---

### Attachment Theory (Adult Relationships) — Bowlby / Ainsworth / Hazan & Shaver
**Core Idea:** Adult romantic attachment styles are shaped by early caregiver relationships.

| Style | Behavior in Relationships |
|-------|--------------------------|
| **Secure** | Comfortable with intimacy and autonomy; good communicator |
| **Anxious-Preoccupied** | Craves closeness; fears abandonment; hypervigilant to cues |
| **Dismissive-Avoidant** | Values independence; uncomfortable with emotional closeness |
| **Fearful-Avoidant** | Wants intimacy but fears it; unpredictable behavior |

> *Attachment style is not destiny — it can change through therapy and healthy relationships.*

---

### Sternberg's Triangular Theory of Love (1986)
**Core Idea:** Love is composed of three components.

```
        Intimacy
       /        \
Passion ———— Commitment
```

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Intimacy** | Warmth, closeness, connection |
| **Passion** | Physical/sexual arousal, romance |
| **Commitment** | Decision to love; long-term dedication |

**Types of Love:**
- Infatuation = Passion only
- Companionate Love = Intimacy + Commitment (no passion)
- Romantic Love = Intimacy + Passion (no commitment)
- Consummate Love = All three ✓ (ideal)
- Empty Love = Commitment only

## 3. Nonverbal Communication in Relationships

**Functions of Nonverbal Communication:**
1. **Complement** verbal messages
2. **Contradict** verbal messages (mixed messages)
3. **Substitute** for verbal messages
4. **Regulate** conversation flow
5. **Accent** or emphasize verbal content

**Key Nonverbal Channels:**
- **Kinesics:** Body movement, gestures, posture
- **Facial expressions:** Universal (Ekman's six: happiness, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, disgust)
- **Eye contact:** Regulates turns, signals interest, dominance
- **Paralanguage:** Tone, pitch, rate, volume, pauses
- **Proxemics (Hall):**
  - Intimate space: 0–18 inches
  - Personal space: 18 in–4 ft
  - Social space: 4–12 ft
  - Public space: 12+ ft
- **Haptics:** Touch — the most intimate nonverbal channel
- **Chronemics:** Use of time as communication

## 4. Active Listening

**The HURIER Model:**
1. **H**earing — physical process
2. **U**nderstanding — cognitive process
3. **R**emembering — retention
4. **I**nterpreting — assigning meaning
5. **E**valuating — critically assessing
6. **R**esponding — providing feedback

**Barriers to Active Listening:**
- Pseudolistening (appearing to listen while distracted)
- Stage-hogging (steering conversation back to self)
- Defensive listening (reacting to perceived attacks)
- Ambushing (listening for ways to counterattack)
- Insulated listening (ignoring uncomfortable information)
