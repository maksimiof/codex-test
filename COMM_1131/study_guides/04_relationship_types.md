## 1. Relationship Development Models

### Knapp's Staircase Model of Relationship Development
A ten-stage model describing how relationships *come together* and *come apart*.

**Coming Together (ascending):**
```
Stage 5: Bonding       ← Formal commitment (marriage, commitment ceremony)
Stage 4: Integrating   ← Identities merge; "couple" identity
Stage 3: Intensifying  ← "I love you"; increased vulnerability
Stage 2: Experimenting ← Small talk; probing for similarities
Stage 1: Initiating    ← First impressions; "Hi, how are you?"
```

**Coming Apart (descending):**
```
Stage 6: Differentiating  ← "I" replaces "we"; emphasizing differences
Stage 7: Circumscribing   ← Reduced topics, reduced depth
Stage 8: Stagnating       ← Communication feels pointless
Stage 9: Avoiding         ← Physical and psychological distance
Stage 10: Terminating     ← End of relationship
```

> **Note:** Relationships don't always follow this sequence; they can move in both directions and skip stages.

---

### Levinger's ABCDE Model
A simpler model of relationship development:

| Stage | Name | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| A | **Attraction** | Initial interest |
| B | **Build-up** | Growing intimacy and interdependence |
| C | **Continuation** | Stable, long-term commitment |
| D | **Deterioration** | Decline in satisfaction |
| E | **Ending** | Dissolution |

## 2. Types of Love and Relationships

### Lee's Color Wheel of Love (1973)
Six love styles (analogous to primary and secondary colors):

**Primary Love Styles:**
- **Eros:** Romantic, passionate love; intense physical/emotional attraction
- **Ludus:** Game-playing love; non-committal, playful
- **Storge:** Friendship-based love; develops gradually

**Secondary Love Styles (combinations):**
- **Pragma (Ludus + Storge):** Practical, logical partner selection
- **Mania (Eros + Ludus):** Obsessive, possessive love; jealousy, anxiety
- **Agape (Eros + Storge):** Unconditional, altruistic love; self-sacrificing

---

### Sternberg's Triangle *(recap from Study Guide 1)*
Consummate Love = Intimacy + Passion + Commitment (all three components)

## 3. Relationship Structures

### Monogamy
- **Serial monogamy:** One partner at a time, sequential relationships
- **Socially monogamous:** Publicly committed to one partner
- **Sexually monogamous:** No sexual activity outside the primary relationship
- Most legally and culturally normative structure in Western societies

### Non-Monogamous Structures

| Structure | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Open relationship** | Primary couple; both may have outside sexual partners |
| **Polyamory** | Multiple loving, romantic relationships with all parties' knowledge/consent |
| **Polyfidelity** | Closed group of 3+ partners; fidelity within the group |
| **Swinging** | Recreational sex with others, usually as a couple activity |
| **Relationship anarchy** | No hierarchy among relationships; each defined individually |

> **Key Principle:** All ethical non-monogamy requires **transparency and consent** from all involved parties.

### Cohabitation
- Living together in an intimate relationship without marriage
- Increasingly common; has risen dramatically since 1970
- Research is mixed on whether it increases divorce risk (selection effect vs. cohabitation effect)

### Marriage
- **Legal contract** with rights and responsibilities
- Same-sex marriage legal in all U.S. states since *Obergefell v. Hodges* (2015)
- Cultural, religious, and legal meanings vary globally

## 4. Relationship Maintenance Behaviors

**Stafford & Canary (1991)** identified five key maintenance strategies:

| Strategy | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Positivity** | Being cheerful, avoiding criticism |
| **Openness** | Direct discussion of the relationship |
| **Assurances** | Expressing commitment; reminding partner you value them |
| **Social Networks** | Spending time with mutual friends |
| **Sharing Tasks** | Dividing household/relationship responsibilities fairly |

---

## 5. Long-Distance Relationships (LDR)

**Challenges:**
- Reduced frequency of physical contact
- Communication primarily through mediated channels
- Managing jealousy and uncertainty
- Idealization of the partner

**What research shows:**
- LDRs are not inherently less satisfying
- Communication *quality* matters more than quantity
- Clear plans for closing the distance reduce uncertainty
- More idealized relationships can cause disillusionment upon reunion

## 6. Relationship Dissolution

**Duck's Model of Relationship Dissolution (1982):**

| Phase | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Intrapsychic** | Individual broods privately; "I can't stand this anymore" |
| **Dyadic** | Confrontation with partner; negotiation or decision to end |
| **Social** | Going public; telling friends/family; social support sought |
| **Grave-dressing** | Creating a narrative about the breakup; moving on |
| **Resurrection** *(added later)* | Rebuilding identity as a single person for future relationships |

## 7. Healthy vs. Unhealthy Relationships

### Signs of a Healthy Relationship:
- Mutual respect and trust
- Open, honest communication
- Support for each other's individual goals
- Fair conflict resolution
- Shared decision-making
- Physical and emotional safety

### Warning Signs / Red Flags:
- Jealousy framed as love
- Isolation from friends and family
- Controlling behavior (finances, appearance, whereabouts)
- Put-downs and humiliation
- Pressure to change core values
- Threats, intimidation

### Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) — Power & Control Wheel
The Duluth Model identifies key tactics used to establish control:
- **Physical violence**
- **Sexual violence**
- Using intimidation, emotional abuse
- Using isolation
- Minimizing, denying, blaming
- Using children
- Using economic abuse
- Using privilege (male privilege framework)
