# Study Guide 3 — Consent, Ethics, and Boundaries

---

## 1. What Is Consent?

**Affirmative Consent:** Active, ongoing, voluntary, and informed agreement to participate in sexual activity.

### The FRIES Model (Planned Parenthood)
A widely used framework for understanding consent:

| Letter | Meaning | Explanation |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **F** | **Freely given** | No pressure, manipulation, threats, or coercion |
| **R** | **Reversible** | Anyone can change their mind at any time |
| **I** | **Informed** | Full knowledge of what is being consented to |
| **E** | **Enthusiastic** | Genuine desire, not just absence of refusal |
| **S** | **Specific** | Yes to one thing ≠ yes to everything |

> **Key Point:** Silence ≠ consent. Passivity ≠ consent. Prior consent ≠ ongoing consent.

---

## 2. What Invalidates Consent?

Consent is **not valid** when:

- The person is **incapacitated** (alcohol, drugs, sleep, unconsciousness)
- There is a **power imbalance** (employer/employee, professor/student, caregiver/patient)
- There is **coercion, threats, or manipulation**
- The person is **underage** (below legal age of consent, which varies by jurisdiction)
- Consent was obtained through **fraud or deception** (e.g., lying about STI status)

## 3. Consent Communication

### Verbal Consent
Direct, explicit language:
- "Yes, I want to."
- "I'd like to try X."
- "Is it okay if I...?"

### Nonverbal Consent Cues
- Nonverbal cues *alone* are insufficient for establishing consent
- Nonverbal discomfort signals to watch for: stiffening, pulling away, silence, averted gaze, freezing

### Ongoing Checking In
- Consent is not a one-time transaction at the start
- Check in during activity: "Does this feel okay?" "Do you want to continue?"

## 4. Sexual Coercion

A spectrum between consensual sex and rape:

```
Consensual ←————————————————————————→ Non-Consensual
   ↑                                         ↑
Enthusiastic                         Assault/Rape
consent          Coercion
                Manipulation
                Pressure
                Threats
```

**Forms of Coercion:**
- Emotional manipulation ("If you loved me, you would...")
- Persistent pressure after a "no"
- Threats of consequences
- Intoxicating someone to lower resistance
- Using authority/power over someone

## 5. Sexual Harassment

**Legal Definition (Title IX, Title VII):**
Unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, or verbal/physical conduct of a sexual nature that:
1. Creates a hostile environment, OR
2. Is made a condition of employment/education (*quid pro quo*)

**Types:**
- **Verbal:** Sexual comments, jokes, requests
- **Nonverbal:** Leering, suggestive gestures
- **Physical:** Unwanted touching
- **Digital/Cyber:** Sexting without consent, sharing intimate images

---

## 6. Rape Culture

**Definition:** A social environment in which sexual violence is normalized, trivializes, or tacitly condoned.

**Manifestations of Rape Culture:**
- Victim-blaming ("She was asking for it")
- Slut-shaming
- Normalizing male entitlement to sex
- Media trivializing sexual violence
- Minimizing or dismissing reports of assault

**Statistics (U.S.):**
- ~1 in 5 women and ~1 in 71 men experience rape in their lifetime (CDC)
- Most sexual assaults are perpetrated by someone the victim knows

## 7. Boundaries in Relationships

**Definition:** Limits that define what behaviors are acceptable and safe for you in a relationship.

### Types of Boundaries:
- **Physical:** Personal space, touch, sexual activity
- **Emotional:** What you share, how you are treated
- **Sexual:** What activities you consent to, protection preferences
- **Digital:** Privacy, sharing photos, social media access
- **Time/Energy:** How much time you dedicate to a relationship

### Communicating Boundaries:
1. Know your own limits
2. Be direct and specific: "I'm not comfortable with X"
3. Expect respect; don't justify, over-explain, or apologize
4. Reinforce consistently
5. Revisit as relationships evolve

### Recognizing Boundary Violations:
- Partner ignores stated limits
- Pressure to do things you're uncomfortable with
- Feeling guilty for setting limits
- Partner dismisses your feelings about violations

## 8. Ethical Frameworks Applied to Sexual Ethics

| Framework | Application |
|-----------|------------|
| **Autonomy (Kantian)** | Respect persons as ends in themselves; never use someone merely as a means |
| **Consequentialism** | Assess the outcomes of sexual behavior for all involved |
| **Care Ethics** | Emphasize relationship, empathy, and responsibility to those we are connected to |
| **Justice** | Examine power structures; protect vulnerable parties |

## 9. Bystander Intervention

**What:** Taking action to interrupt situations that could lead to sexual harassment or assault.

**The 3 D's Model:**
1. **Direct:** Step in directly — "Hey, leave them alone"
2. **Distract:** Create a distraction — spill a drink, ask for directions
3. **Delegate:** Get help from a third party — security, RA, friend

**When to intervene:** When you observe warning signs — someone being isolated, intoxicated beyond capacity to consent, or being pressured.
