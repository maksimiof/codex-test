# Study Guide 6 — Conflict and Communication Styles

---

## 1. What Is Conflict?

**Definition:** A perceived incompatibility of goals, values, needs, or behaviors between two or more interdependent people.

**Key Word: "Perceived"** — Conflict exists when at least one party believes incompatibility exists, regardless of whether it is "real."

### Is Conflict Always Negative?
**No.** Conflict can be:
- **Functional:** Leads to growth, problem-solving, increased understanding
- **Dysfunctional:** Destructive; damages relationship or fails to address the issue

---

## 2. Sources of Conflict in Relationships

| Source | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **Content conflict** | Disagreement about facts or information |
| **Value conflict** | Differing core beliefs or priorities |
| **Ego/face conflict** | Argument escalates to personal attacks; self-image threatened |
| **Relational conflict** | Disagreement about the relationship itself ("You never listen to me") |
| **Meta-conflict** | Conflict about *how* conflict is handled ("You always shut down") |

---

## 3. Communication/Conflict Styles

### Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Instrument (TKI)
Two dimensions: **Assertiveness** (concern for self) × **Cooperativeness** (concern for others)

```
                High Assertiveness
                      |
          Competing   |   Collaborating
                      |
Low Cooperation ——————+—————— High Cooperation
                      |
          Avoiding    |   Accommodating
                      |
                Low Assertiveness
                      (Compromising is in the center)
```

| Style | Description | When Appropriate |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Competing** | High assertiveness, low cooperation; "win/lose" | Emergencies; when you must stand firm |
| **Collaborating** | High assertiveness + high cooperation; "win/win" | Complex issues; important long-term relationships |
| **Compromising** | Moderate on both; each gives something | When time is limited; equal-power situation |
| **Accommodating** | Low assertiveness, high cooperation; "I'll give in" | Issue is trivial to you; relationship preservation is priority |
| **Avoiding** | Low on both; withdrawal | Issue is trivial; cooling down period needed |

## 4. Gottman's "Four Horsemen" of Relationship Apocalypse

John Gottman's research identified four communication patterns that predict relationship dissolution with ~90% accuracy.

| Horseman | Description | Antidote |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Criticism** | Attacking the *person* (not the behavior): "You're so selfish" | Gentle start-up; "I statements" |
| **Contempt** | Superiority, mockery, disrespect, eye-rolling | Build culture of appreciation |
| **Defensiveness** | Deflecting blame; counter-attacking | Take responsibility |
| **Stonewalling** | Shutting down, withdrawing, "the silent treatment" | Physiological self-soothing; take breaks |

> **Most destructive:** Contempt — the single best predictor of divorce.

## 5. "I" Statements vs. "You" Statements

| "You" Statement (accusatory) | "I" Statement (assertive) |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------|
| "You never listen to me" | "I feel unheard when I'm interrupted" |
| "You're always late" | "I feel anxious when you're late without a text" |
| "You make me so angry" | "I feel angry when the dishes aren't done" |

**"I" Statement Formula:**
> "I feel [emotion] when [specific behavior] because [impact on me]. I would prefer [desired change]."

---

## 6. Active Listening in Conflict

**Strategies:**
1. **Paraphrase:** Restate what you heard in your own words; check for accuracy
2. **Perception check:** "I get the impression you're frustrated — is that right?"
3. **Emotional validation:** "That sounds really hard." (Does not mean you agree)
4. **Ask open-ended questions:** "Can you help me understand what you mean by...?"

**What NOT to do during conflict:**
- Interrupt
- Plan your rebuttal while the other person is talking
- Bring up unrelated past grievances ("kitchen sinking")
- Escalate tone or volume
- Make ultimatums without intent to follow through

---

## 7. Assertiveness vs. Passivity vs. Aggression

| Style | Behavior | Message Sent |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| **Passive** | Avoids expressing needs; goes along to avoid conflict | "My needs don't matter" |
| **Aggressive** | Expresses needs at others' expense; hostile | "Your needs don't matter" |
| **Assertive** | Expresses needs directly while respecting others | "Both of our needs matter" |
| **Passive-aggressive** | Indirect expression of anger (silent treatment, backhanded comments) | Mixed/confusing messages |

## 8. Apologies and Forgiveness

### Components of an Effective Apology (Gary Chapman & Jennifer Thomas):
1. **Express regret:** "I am sorry"
2. **Accept responsibility:** "I was wrong"
3. **Make restitution:** "What can I do to make it right?"
4. **Genuinely repent:** "I'll try not to do this again"
5. **Request forgiveness:** "Will you forgive me?"

### Forgiveness ≠ Reconciliation
- Forgiveness is an internal process of releasing resentment — it benefits *you*
- Reconciliation requires change from both parties and may not always be appropriate or safe

---

## 9. Negotiation and Compromise

**Principled Negotiation (Fisher & Ury, *Getting to Yes*):**
1. **Separate people from the problem** — attack issues, not each other
2. **Focus on interests, not positions** — "What do you need?" not just "What do you want?"
3. **Generate multiple options** — brainstorm before evaluating
4. **Use objective criteria** — agree on fair standards for evaluation

---

## 10. Digital Communication and Conflict

**Challenges of Conflict via Text/Messaging:**
- Tone is ambiguous; easy to misread
- Faster escalation without nonverbal cues
- Permanence — screenshots can escalate
- "Typing" indicators increase anxiety

**Best Practices:**
- Move serious conflicts to face-to-face or at minimum phone/video
- Avoid texting during high emotions
- Don't interpret tone without checking in
